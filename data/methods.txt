To model the spatiotemporal patterns of complaints, we implemented a CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP) tensor decomposition framework to capture the multi-dimensional structure of the data of community board, complaint type, and time. This approach was selected to extract the latent, intersecting patterns among these three dimensions. With over 6 million records, more than 70 community boards, and 156 months of data - computational efficiency was a key consideration. We leveraged the TensorLy library with a PyTorch backend for faster computations and improved scalability. The optimal tensor rank was identified using an elbow analysis of Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) scores to balance model fit and complexity. Once decomposed, the temporal (time in months) factor matrix was forecasted 12 months ahead using an exponential smoothing model (ETS). The simplicity of the ETS model enabled efficient, in-memory short-term forecasts that were then folded back into the reconstructed tensor to estimate complaint volumes for those respective 12 months. This efficient and scalable framework allows us to repeat the process on expanded datasets with additional explanatory features like event permit or construction data.
