As the largest city by both population and urban area in the United States, New York City (NYC) is a densely populated environment where public transportation, nightlife, shopping, construction, and vibrant neighborhoods constantly overlap, making it nearly impossible for residents to escape the sounds of the city. To help residents navigate the challenges of urban life and access city services efficiently, NYC launched the 311 system (NYC311) in 2003, consolidating over 40 different agency hotlines into a single platform for non-emergency requests. Available 24/7, 365 days a year, NYC311 allows residents to report issues ranging from potholes to tree litter and has handled over 500 million calls as of 2023. The NYC311 system has provided a vital way for residents to make concerns about quality-of-life issues heard and addressed across the range of NYC agencies. 

Among more than 500 Service Request types through NYC311, noise complaints are especially frequent and revealing as documenting potential violations of NYC’s Noise Code, which “defines limits on ambient noise levels throughout the city.” Prolonged exposure to loud noise is known to have lasting, negative effects on human hearing (Mehrotra et al., 2024) and in NYC, noise also reflects broader questions of equity, as complaint patterns often align with socioeconomic disparities (Ramphal et al., 2022). In fact, the very first 311 call in March 2003 was a noise complaint “about a loud party in Jackson Heights,” highlighting the prominent role of noise in residents’ lives and their interactions with city services. This study can be a useful tool for the public and decision makers to better understand the noise pattern and the associated external and internal costs for residents living in urban environments.
